The first concept to grasp as you start your journey as a developer is that of input/process/output. These three words sum up the structure of computers and programs. The input/process/output, or IPO, model is a widely used approach in systems analysis and software development for describing a process or a program. The IPO model is the most basic structure for describing a process.

Let’s look at a simple example of input/process/output. When you go to the bank to deposit or withdraw money, you will find that input/process/output is at play. You, yourself, will provide the input portion of our analogy. For our example, let’s say that you are depositing money. You communicate to the teller what you would like done and give them the money to deposit into an account. The teller will process the money you input, carefully counting it for the deposit. Finally, once the money has been processed, the teller will give you a receipt. The receipt is the output of your transaction at the bank.

As you can see in this example, the idea of input/process/output can be found in everyday tasks. Take a moment and think of where else in your life this idea is present. This idea is evident in just about every aspect of our lives and is especially true when it comes to writing computer programs. Every application that you will work on holds to the idea of input/process/output.
